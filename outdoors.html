Diet

Disclaimer: I'm by no measure a dietician or even familar with a fraction of the things dietitions concern themselves with; and so the information I provide below is very surface level from quick google searches I made, and so I'm sure a devout could point out nuances in what I claim below. Even still, I belive my surface level understanding will suffice for my measly purposes; and if there's something royally wrong and you feel the need to inform me, add me on discord (contact tab).

Our bodies require 6 different nutrient groups to sustain proper function:
> Carbohydrates: These for us is what fuel is to a car. To expand, the entries below would be the engine oil, brake fluid etc. So these are where we get our energy from. There are simple and complex carbohydrates. The former spikes bood suger rapidly, while the latter tries to find an equilibrium. Generally, complex carbohydrates are better for us. Foods that are high in simple carbs are low is every other department include candies; they are nearly 100% carbs by weight.
> Proteins: These are cental to our bodies ability to repair damaged tissue -- including organ tissue. They create antibodies which in turn help us fight of viruses. We can categorize proteins into 2 different types, complete proteins (contain 9 essential amino acids) and incomplete proteins (lack 1 or more essential amino acids). Foods which contain the former include, meat, fish, eggs, dairy, and soy. Containing the latter include, beans, lentils, and grains.
> Fats: These help protect organs and aid in the absorbtion of nutrients. There are three categories, unsaturated, saturated, and trans fats. Unsaturated fats are benefical for the heart and cholesterol levels; foods that include them are, olive oil, avocados, salmon, flaxseeds, and walnuts; the last 3 incude fatty acids which are known to help play a role in lessening the adverse effects of ADHD and are generally needed for the proper function of the brain. Saturated fats raise cholesterol in when consumed in excess and can cause heart issues. Sources of staurated fats are: fatty meat, butter, cheese, whole milk, and coconut oil. Saturated fat intake under 10% of your daily calories. Trans-fats should be avoided at ALL costs. Common sources include, deep-fried foods, fast food, and some baked goods. Note that unsaturated fats are the only fats we need to injest. Saturated fats are not essential as our bodies are able to produce it internally.
> Vitamins: These help our cells thrive. There are 13 essential ones, and they can be categorized into 2 -- fat soluble and water soluble vitamins. The former do not need to be consumed everday and the latter do. Fat soluble vitamins: A - milk, eggs, and orange or dark green vegetables; D - sunlight exposure, rainbow trout, wild salmon, and herring; E - walnuts; K - spinach and kale. Water soluble vitamins (everyday): C - fruits, peppers, and tomatoes; B1 - whole grains, pork, and walnuts; B2 - milk, beef liver and other animal organs; B3 - salmon; B5 - cooked mushrooms, avocados, whole grains, and meat; B6 - yellow-fin tuna, Wild Atlantic salmon, sockeye salmon, potatoes, and avocados; B7 - eggs and salmon; B9 - leafy greens and beans;
> Minerals: These can be effectively treated as vitamins for my shallow purposes. The macromineral minerals (large quantities) we need are: calcium (milk, cheese, balkan yogurt), phosphorus (<--), magnesium (walnuts, spinach), sodium (salt), potassium (baked potato with skin), chloride (salt), sulfur (eggs). Trace minerals we need are, iron (beef liver, beef, chicken), zinc (salmon), copper (beef liver, sunflower seeds), iodine (salmon), selenium (yellow-fin tuna), chromium (whole grains), fluride (black tea) and manganese (whole grains).
